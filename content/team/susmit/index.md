I'm an Associate Professor and Graduate Director in Computer Science at Tennessee Tech.

At NGIN Lab, our goal is to build networks and systems that think, sense, adapt, and heal by themselves. Our work spans AI-embedded distributed systems, exascale network measurement, and immersive AR/VR networking. We're also designing and building TREN, Tennessee's first dedicated research and education network connecting universities statewide. The lab has graduated 18 students and currently hosts 6 PhD students, 1 Master's student, and 1 REU student.

Since joining Tennessee Tech in 2019, I've secured $5.0M in research funding and $4.6M in teaching and service funding across multiple NSF, State, and Federal awards. Our work has produced 60+ peer-reviewed publications (and counting). I've received the university's Wings Up 100 Award in 2021, 2022, and 2023. I'm a Senior Member of IEEE and have served as General Chair of IEEE CloudNet, TPC Chair for IEEE ANTS and the INFOCOM CNERT workshop, and I serve on the editorial board of several journals.

Prior to Tennessee Tech, I was a Graduate Research Assistant at Colorado State University, where I received my PhD in Computer Science in 2019, advised by Profs. Christos Papadopoulos and Craig Partridge. My dissertation, *The Future of Networking is the Future of Large Data*, grew out of software I helped deploy and test at CERN, Caltech, LBNL, and Clemson. I also spent time as a research intern at CableLabs and Lawrence Berkeley National Lab.

I'm also active in teaching — I regularly teach Computer Networking (CSC 4200/5200) and Advanced Network Security at Tennessee Tech, and received an Open Educational Resources grant from the Tennessee Board of Regents to build free, open-source course materials for our Computer Networks course.
